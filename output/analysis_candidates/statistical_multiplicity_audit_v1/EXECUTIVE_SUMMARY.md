# Statistical Rescue Audit — Executive Summary

## Bottom line

The original keyboard discovery is internally reproducible, but it does not support a confirmatory claim on the current cohort. The original screen contained 204 T1 tests and 204 longitudinal change tests, with Benjamini–Hochberg adjustment applied within each 204-test scope. The three headline longitudinal Executive Function associations had original q-values of .080, .090, and .154, so none crossed q<.05.

A dependence-aware maximum-statistic permutation calibration across all 204 longitudinal tests gave FWER-adjusted p-values of approximately .341 for correction-recovery/Executive Function, .323 for afternoon IEI/Executive Function, and .694 for IEI-CV/Executive Function. These are exploratory calibration results, not confirmatory evidence, but they show that the headline p-values are not robust to the full-screen multiplicity burden.

The strongest current hypothesis remains afternoon 12:00–18:00 snapshot-derived IEI change versus Executive Function change. Its discovery estimate is rho=.593 (n=28), but the broader technical-factor adjustment attenuates it to partial rho=.356 (p=.113). In the same cohort, IEI change is related to active-day change, interval-count change, event rate, long-pause rate, and device/app composition. Twenty-two of 28 participants changed device set. The result is therefore a technically qualified measurement hypothesis, not evidence of clean typing-speed change.

## What is defensible now

- Reproducible exploratory associations with exact source-family q-values and bootstrap intervals.
- A transparent account of feature dependence, coverage, device/app composition, and snapshot semantics.
- A prespecified future validation target, provided it is tested in independent participants with frozen features and windows.

## What is not defensible now

- Calling any current finding a validated biomarker.
- Treating grouped or post-selection q-values as confirmatory evidence.
- Describing snapshot IEI as direct keystroke timing.
- Presenting the current cohort as an independent validation sample.

## Recommended supervisor decision

Keep one primary exploratory hypothesis—afternoon IEI change versus Executive Function change—and report the other selected signals as secondary/supplementary. For confirmation, use a new participant cohort with approximately 100–120 usable participants for a three-hypothesis family if the plausible effect is around |rho|=.35, allowing for coverage loss and uncertainty. A reanalysis of the present cohort cannot provide independent confirmation.
